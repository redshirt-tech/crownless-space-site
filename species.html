Xenodatabase

Cooperative Civilizations

The galaxy is a big place, and while intelligent civilizations are somewhat rare compared to the number of stars and planets, there are still a wide array of different space-faring peoples that have been encountered by the Cooperative.

Humans

Their homeworld is Earth, the third planet in the Sol system. Earth still has the scars of ecological disaster and nuclear war from years past, but the Earthlings have made great strides in regenerating their biosphere and ecosystem. Earth has a substantial variety of biomes, and humans have learned to adapt to most of them. Their adaptability allows them to thrive in other planetary environments, and there are human communities on many of the Cooperative worlds.

Humans are curious, social, and industrious. Most of them grew up in a society waging an indefinite war against a largely unseen enemy, only manifesting as friends and family failing to return from a deep space mission. Since the end of the war, many have turned away from space, preferring to enjoy more mundane, terrestrial affairs. Still, some feel the pull towards the unknown, and find their purpose among the stars.

Lyrans

Originally from a star system in the Lyran constellation, the Lyrans were given an ancient warning about the Zookeeper machines and managed to escape their world before the Zookeepers could assemble a proper extermination fleet. The Lyran migration went in several different directions in the hope that it would give them a better chance for their civilization to survive. One of those migration fleets came to Earth just as the Zookeepers were about to exterminate humanity. Humans and Lyrans joined to form the Interstellar Cooperative, and together they fought a war to rid the Zookeepers from their region of space.

The Lyran homeworld was covered almost entirely with water, with a single, thin archipelago crossing much of the circumference of the planet. Their society developed around moving from island to island, trading goods and knowledge, occasionally waging wars or plundering, but eventually finding a shared homeostasis that allowed them to flourish as a single people. From there, they were able to advance their technology and unlock many of the secrets of the universe. They eventually discovered the remains of a previous civilization on their homeworld, destroyed by the Zookeepers millennia ago, still waiting in the deep dark to strike them down if they reached too far into the heavens. The Lyrans made a plan of escape, and eventually some made it to Earth and the founding of the Cooperative.

The Lyran migration to Sol saw many of their people settle on various island regions on Earth, reestablishing a link to their terrestrial and aquatic origins, but others had become acclimatized to life aboard spacecraft and orbital habitats. As a result, though their overall population is small relative to humans, they are overrepresented in space.

Lyrans are humanoid, usually taller and more slender than humans, with long legs and long arms. Their skin ranges from dark to light, but always with a slight purple hue. Their eyes range from dull gold to deep red. They tend to wear their hair long, though some have adopted shorter styles from humans. They tend to be practical, decisive, and frugal. Living on spaceships, counting calories and cubic inches of air to keep an entire civilization alive, leaves little room for idleness or frivolity. Since arriving at Sol, some have "gone native" and embraced a more laid-back terrestrial lifestyle, but the average Lyran is still very conscious of the dangers of the Void.

Solarans

When the Great Mobilization against the Zookeeper threat began, Humans and Lyrans worked together very closely. Many formed romantic relationships, and wanted to have children. A genetic engineering program was undertaken to create a hybrid species of Humans and Lyrans so that mixed-species couples could have children. This was seen as very controversial by the more conservative members of both civilizations, but many saw it as a profound symbol of unity.

There are Solarans scattered throughout Human and Lyran populations, but the largest concentration is on the Solar Statite, a space station that floats above the Sun's north pole. Here the Solarans draw raw materials from the Sun and are building a home for themselves and their kind. They have become expert shipwrights, maintaining the practice of weaving hyperdiamond into ultra-resilient hulls fit for deep space. Their belief is that there will always be another threat to the flourishing of sentient life, and so they must always be prepared for the next war.

Arcturans

The Arcturans were the first intelligent species encountered by the Cooperative fleet as they cleared nearby space of Zookeepers. The Arcturans were initially suspicious of the Cooperative, but the fleet showed them the Zookeeper threat first-hand and they quickly changed their attitude. They were very close to working out the Grand Unified Theory and experimenting with negative energy manipulation, making themselves a Zookeeper target. Arcturans joined the Cooperative shortly after contact and were instrumental in the ongoing war effort.

Arcturus IV, the Arcturan homeworld, is a rocky, geologically active planet with a thin but breathable atmosphere on the surface. It has an abundance of caves, tunnels, and caverns, not to mention vast underground oceans, teeming with life. The geology of the planet results in the rocks releasing a steady supply of oxygen, and numerous geothermal vents provide ample energy for the extremophiles that underpin a rich, diverse ecosystem. The caves, though vast by Earth standards, were still very limited, and there were many dangerous creatures living in the depths.

Arcturans had to literally carve themselves an existence from the rocks, tunneling and excavating to create large underground cities in geologically stable areas. Evolving in these conditions has made Arcturans excellent spacefarers, as they are accustomed to cramped spaces, filtered air, and artificial lighting. Arcturan engineers are renowned and sought-after for their skills in keeping complex systems running under extreme conditions.

They are humanoids, usually shorter than Humans or Lyrans, with course, ashen skin, and white hair. They have pale eyes that are usually silver-grey to ice blue, and are much larger than their other features. Their society is highly social, sharing space and possessions freely, and with complex relationship networks. Reputation is the central currency, as it was important to be well-liked and get along when space and security are always at a premium. They appreciate the Cooperative's orderly, egalitarian principles, quickly seeing the benefits in joining the aliens from the stars on their mission.

Ranians

The Ranians are a semi-nomadic hunter-gatherer civilization organized into a loose federation of clans spread across the world. When the Ranians were encountered by the Cooperative, they wanted nothing to do with spacefarers. Their oral history, passed down for untold millennia, cautioned against delving into the secrets of the universe or going to the stars for fear of drawing attention from the evil spirits that haunt the sky.

The Cooperative xenoculturalists tried to explain their mission, but there was deep distrust. Eventually they were able to establish a rapport with a clan of Ranians by driving away a group of pirates who came to the planet looking for slaves. Since then, Rana has become a Cooperative protectorate, and some Ranians have formally joined the Cooperative, although most still live the traditional Ranian lifestyle.

Rana III is a lush, biologically diverse planet. The largest continental landmass sits mostly within the equatorial region, and is a gradient of forests, jungles, and savanna. There are a handful of smaller landmasses in the colder regions that are less hospitable, and so most of the Ranian population lives across the main continent.

Ranians have only primitive technology, but they have delved deeply into spiritual practices and mental discipline. They have very powerful telepaths, who act as shamans and oracles, helping to ease illness or navigate local crises. Cooperative telepaths have made pilgrimages to study with the Ranians, although gaining their trust is an involved process.

Porrimans

The Porriman live on a serene, largely pastoral world. Like Earth, it is mostly ocean, with a couple of large continents. The largest landmasses are covered mostly with grasslands and low foothills with extensive river networks, which is where most of the Porriman communities emerged. Much of its heat comes from the gas giant that it orbits, which maintains the entire planet at a fairly even temperature, augmented somewhat by the star Porrima, which is smaller and cooler than Sol. The planet's environment doesn't experience much variation compared to many other worlds, and the Porriman are accustomed to very gradual and minor seasonal shifts. Large herbivores roam the land, many of which have been domesticated for agricultural purposes.

Porriman society is a loose federation of farming guilds and families who traded goods back and forth with one another for millennia. They never bothered to develop advanced technology, and were quite surprised when the Cooperative visited them and told them about the wider universe. Many younger Porriman joined the Cooperative, with older Porriman showing great concern for their young people leaving or forming Cooperative communities with vastly different technologies and lifestyles, but after a generation or so, most of the farming guilds agreed to reform under the Cooperative banner. Porriman society has seen a great deal of change in recent years, but most agree that it has been beneficial.

Porriman are shorter than humans, very stout, and very hairy. They have large facial features and thick, strong limbs. They tend to be casual in their interactions, very hard-working in professional situations, and enjoy idle chatter. They tend to be communal in nature, preferring consensus over authority, and deliberation over conflict. Some Porriman have embraced the changes since the Cooperative contacted them, automating of their previous agricultural pursuits and encouraging their children to seek education and understanding of their new and much larger universe. Others are traditionalists, and have formed a non-Cooperative union of their own that eschews modern technology. For this reason, Porriman has become a hub for traditionalist factions across the Cooperative, such as the Amish of Earth, or Markaban bedouin.

Markabans

Markab II is a hot, dry world with relatively little water on the surface and an abundance of rough terrain such as mountains, canyons, and deserts. There are a number of oases regions that host most of the biodiversity. These oasis regions are highly competitive environments where any species could be supplanted and pushed to the desert if they couldn't keep up. On the outskirts of these regions, deadly predators developed to prey on the creatures within the oases, living in the desert but hunting in the savannahs and jungles closer to water sources, or hunting the weaker desert creatures.

The Markabans evolved from a species that was pushed to the margins of an oasis, but developed social structures and larger brains that let them cooperate and hunt more formidable creatures, in both the desert and oasis. They soon became the dominant species, and migrated to all oasis regions. From there, Markaban society was defined by control of these regions, which routinely raided one another, fought wars, and competed for access to resources in the deep desert. As society advanced, the oases, or Wetlands, were viewed as somewhat sacred and were spared much of the turmoil in the Drylands outside of these regions. Markabans often identified themselves as Wetlanders or Drylanders, depending on their orientation towards living and working in the more structured and peaceful oasis regions vs the more dangerous and chaotic deserts.

When the Cooperative arrived, the Markabans had advanced technology. They were in a state of precarious stability, depending on a doctrine of mutually assured destruction among the Wetlands regions while allowing the Drylanders to govern themselves without oversight. The Markab initially viewed the Cooperative as invaders, refusing to believe their stories about the Zookeepers or their mission.

The Markabans secretly united against the Cooperative, launching a surprise attack on the Cooperative ships in orbit, capturing them for themselves. They waged a brief war against the rest of the Cooperative fleet to drive them out of the system. Shortly thereafter, a nascent Zookeeper infestation, left to grow by the Markabans who thought they were a ruse, attacked Markab and destroyed most of the Markab fleet. The Markabans reestablished contact with the Cooperative, who sent their fleet to eliminate the Zookeeper threat and establish a presence in the system to ensure no further incidents occurred. From there, Markab society came around to the Cooperative ways, having seen that the Cooperative could have destroyed them at any time, and even came to their aid despite their belligerence. The new united Markab reformed under the Cooperative, and Markabans began serving in the Cooperative fleet. These spacefaring Markabans were a mixture of Wetlanders and Drylanders, often referred to collectively as Voidlanders.

Markabans are tall, broad, and covered in scaly skin. They have hardened scales on their heads, elbows, knees, and knuckles, and they have large, rather sharp teeth. These features are often more or less prominent depending on whether the individual is primarily descended from Drylanders or Wetlanders. Drylanders tend to be larger, with thicker scales and bigger teeth, while Wetlanders are the opposite. There are plenty of Markabans of mixed heritage nowadays, although that was not always as socially accepted. Markabans tend to be competitive, outspoken, and quick to act. They prefer dealing with conflict openly, but are willing to use any means necessary to achieve their goals if they believe they are in the right.

Capellans

Capella VII is world with a cold but habitable climate, with the land area mostly covered in ice, permafrost, and boreal forest. The climate falls below zero about six out of every eight months, cycling between a long, deep winter and a brisk, cool spring season. Many land-based lifeforms evolved to hibernate or go dormant during the coldest part of the year, while others had lifecycles that were purely seasonal, laying eggs in late spring that would hatch in the early spring to repeat the cycle.

The Capellans evolved from scavengers, who live year-round by gathering eggs and hunting dormant creatures during the late spring and early winter, saving these finds in underground caches, and living off the stored food in natural shelters through the coldest part of the winter. The Capellans were unique in that they learned that they could ensure a safer and more bountiful spring by choosing which creatures they harvested. By culling dangerous creatures and their eggs while allowing more docile creatures and their eggs to make it through the winter, they were able to control their ecosystem in their favor.

This strategy required a great deal of cooperation, which led to the Capellans developing a sophisticated communal society. The long winter months indoors gave Capellans a chance to study and create art, music, philosophy, and eventually science and technology. When the Cooperative encountered them, they had already worked out the existence of the Zookeepers and the inherent danger they posed, but decided to avoid the issue by not developing interstellar technology. They were happy to join the Cooperative, and now there are many Capellans serving across Cooperative space.

Capellans are humanoids covered in thick fur in a variety of colors. They are quiet, methodical, and disciplined, often taking a great deal of time and deliberation to make decisions. They have long-since escaped the seasonal cycle of their world by building climate-controlled habitats, but they still have an inherent understanding of ecosystems and interconnected systems in general, which makes them excellent scientists and medical professionals.

Denebians

Deneb was similar to old Earth in many ways, a lush paradise with many diverse biomes teeming with complex life. However, the Denebians, in much the same fashion as the Humans and the Glesians, consumed their planetary resources, poisoned their environment, and shattered their society with armed conflict, ending with global mutually-assured destruction through nuclear war. What remained is a shell of its former abundant greenery, with large swaths of the planet unlivable toxic, and the rest only marginally habitable.

The Cooperative noticed the Denebians from the large spike in radioactive emissions caused by hundreds of rapid nuclear explosions. The emissions were only detected years after the disaster, and the Cooperative arrived to find a planet in the depths of extreme collapse. Social institutions had ceased to function, leaving room for gangs and warlords to brutally exploit the remains of civilization with abominable violence. The scout ship determined that the Denebians would continue to suffer indefinitely, with a significant chance of total extinction within a couple of generations.

The Cooperative redirected every spare ship in the fleet to send food, supplies, medicine, and an army of volunteers and service members to deliver aid and stabilize the remnants of the planet's society. Many Denebians were grateful, but the new rulers of Deneb fought to keep what they had, and the Cooperative service was forced to deploy peacekeepers to maintain the safety of aid workers and liberated Denebians. Fierce debate was had within the Cooperative about how to approach the Deneb crisis, but in the end it was decided that proactive intervention was necessary. The Cooperative came in force and used the full breadth of their technological and military superiority to end the tyranny.

Deneb became a protectorate of the Earth government until the immediate crisis was over and Deneb had a stable ecological and economic base to work from. Most Denebians joined the Cooperative during this time, either to leave the planet or to contribute to the rebuilding. Deneb is still suffering from the physical and psychological scars from its recent history, but things are looking brighter.

Denebians are humanoids, not dissimilar from Humans, Lyrans, and the like. Most are only a generation, at most, removed form the collapse, and many Denebians have trauma from that time. However, they also tend to be resourceful, resilient, and very observant. They don't like waste or idleness, and see much of the Cooperative as being somewhat complacent. Many have left to serve in the fleet in order to forget what happened. There is a movement on Deneb to return Denebians to Deneb as the rebuilding efforts make progress, which is a matter of ongoing discussion within Denebian families with members serving away.

Lacertans

Lacerta is a rocky, mountainous planet with severe weather and ancient civilization, predating other Cooperative civilizations by many millennia.

Non-Cooperative Civilizations

The Cephalopoid Alliance

During the New Renaissance era of Humanity's history, there was a de-extinction movement that endeavored to bring back numerous animal species that were lost or severely underpopulated due to ecological damage. With the de-extinction efforts came a great deal of research and experimentation with genetic engineering, and humanity became quite adept at designing and modifying life to fit within ecological niches once left vacant or underrepresented.

An offshoot of the de-extinction movement sought not only to balance the ecosystem, but also to use this growing knowledge to bring sentience to lifeforms on the cusp of true consciousness. This was viewed broadly as overstepping the boundaries, going beyond correcting past mistakes and into playing God, but nonetheless a few underground fanatics began experimenting with enhancing animal intelligence to create new sentient species.

Initially, the most successful results came with the cephalopods, who grew their capabilities with minimal modification required. However, after a couple of generations, they became hostile to the newly-uplifted cetaceans, fighting an underwater war for supremacy. The humans did their best to protect the cetaceans and keep the peace, eventually brokering a peace and causing the cephalopods to retreat.

The cephalopods were rarely encountered afterwards until the Great Mobilization. When space travel became common, a cephalopod army materialized out of the ocean, occupied a space elevator, and commandeered several starships. The uplifted cephalopods left the Sol system and warned humanity that they are not ready to follow.

In recent years there have been sightings of Cephalopod vessels, resembling starship-sized nautiloids. They have been reported occasionally trading with nearby civilizations and fighting off pirates in their territory, but formal diplomatic relations have not yet been established between them and the Cooperative.

The Felisian Imperium

The Felisians were a feudal society a thousand years ago when a spaceship from elsewhere crash landed on their planet. The Felisians worshipped the ship as a God, building a great temple around it, and the Order of the Fallen Star was born. The clergy learned from the ship, using its database and reverse-engineering its technology. They used their knowledge to rule the Felisian people for hundreds of years. Eventually they got too greedy, and an uprising toppled them, led by several Clans who formed a global revolutionary force. They purged the world of the clergy and the Order, forming the Imperium as the new ruling body.

They used the knowledge uncovered from the Fallen Star to quickly industrialize, building a fleet of ships to pre-emptively destroy the dormant Zookeepers within their star system, then beginning their exploration of deep space in earnest.

They found several other alien civilizations, making contact and offering them protection in exchange for fealty.
