Xenodatabase

Cooperative Civilizations

Humans

Homeworld: Earth (Sol III), still scarred but regenerating. Curious, social, industrious, and highly adaptable — human communities exist on many Cooperative worlds. Most grew up during the war against a largely unseen enemy; many have turned away from space, but some still feel the pull of the unknown.

Lyrans

Escaped their water-covered homeworld ahead of Zookeeper extermination, warned by an ancient message buried beneath the surface. Decades of flight ended at Earth, where their fleet destroyed the first Zookeeper swarm and formal contact was made. Taller and more slender than humans, skin with a purple hue, eyes dull gold to deep red. Practical, decisive, frugal — spaceship life leaves little room for frivolity — and overrepresented in space relative to their numbers.

Solarans

An engineered Human–Lyran hybrid species, born of the Great Mobilization so mixed couples could have children — controversial to conservatives, a profound symbol of unity to others. Largest concentration: the Solar Statite floating above the Sun's north pole. Expert shipwrights who weave hyperdiamond hulls; they believe there will always be another threat, so one must always be prepared.

Arcturans

First species contacted during the clearing of nearby space; they were close to unlocking negative energy themselves (a Zookeeper target). From the cave cities of geologically active Arcturus IV: shorter than humans, coarse ashen skin, white hair, large pale eyes. Accustomed to cramped spaces, filtered air, artificial light — renowned engineers. Highly social; reputation is their central currency.

Ranians

Semi-nomadic hunter-gatherer clans of lush Rana III whose oral history warns against the stars and the evil spirits that haunt the sky. Rapport was won when the Cooperative drove off slaver pirates; Rana is now a protectorate. Primitive technology, but very powerful telepaths — shamans and oracles with whom Cooperative telepaths make pilgrimages to study.

Porrimans

From a serene pastoral world warmed by its gas giant. Short, stout, hairy; casual, hard-working, communal — consensus over authority. Farming guilds gradually reformed under the Cooperative. Porrima has become the hub for traditionalist factions across the Cooperative (Earth Amish, Markaban bedouin, and its own non-Cooperative traditionalist union).

Markabans

From hot, dry Markab II — oasis "Wetlands" vs. desert "Drylands" shaped their society (spacefarers are "Voidlanders"). They ambushed the Cooperative fleet at first contact, then were nearly destroyed by a Zookeeper infestation they'd dismissed as a ruse; the Cooperative saved them anyway, and Markab came around. Tall, broad, scaly, with hardened plates and sharp teeth. Competitive, outspoken, quick to act — open about conflict, but willing to use any means if they believe they're in the right.

Capellans

From cold Capella VII, evolved from scavengers who learned to curate their ecosystem through selective winter harvesting — cooperation that built a sophisticated communal society rich in art and science. They had already deduced the Zookeepers' existence and chose not to develop interstellar technology. Fur-covered humanoids; quiet, methodical, disciplined. Their instinct for interconnected systems makes them superb scientists and medics.

Denebians

Deneb destroyed itself in nuclear war; the Cooperative arrived to find collapse, warlords, and looming extinction, and intervened in force — the subject of fierce internal debate. Most Denebians joined during the rebuilding. Resourceful, resilient, observant; intolerant of waste and idleness, and many see the Cooperative as complacent. A movement to return Denebians home is an ongoing question in families with members serving away.

Non-Cooperative Civilizations

The Cephalopoid Alliance

Uplifted cephalopods — a product of humanity's New Renaissance de-extinction and uplift experiments. After warring with the uplifted cetaceans and retreating, they reappeared during the Great Mobilization: an army out of the ocean, a space elevator occupied, several starships commandeered. They left Sol with a warning that humanity is not ready to follow. Their nautiloid-shaped vessels are occasionally seen trading or fighting pirates in their territory; no formal diplomatic relations exist.

The Felisian Imperium

A thousand years ago, a spaceship of unknown origin — the Fallen Star — crash-landed on feudal Felisia. The Order of the Fallen Star worshipped and reverse-engineered it, ruling for centuries until a clan-led uprising purged them and formed the Imperium. Using the Fallen Star's knowledge they industrialized rapidly, pre-emptively destroyed the dormant Zookeepers in their system, and expanded — offering other civilizations protection in exchange for fealty.

The Voidchosen

Formally the Church of the Chosen of the Infinite Void, a religion from Capella VI. They claim their society has been shepherded for millennia by angels from the stars, who decreed that some must be chosen to go to the stars to defend the homeworld from demons.
